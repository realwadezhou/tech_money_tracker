How these numbers are counted

Every quarterly lobbying report lists the issues lobbied on, each with a short description written by the filer. An issue entry is counted for a topic when one of the topic's phrases appears in its description. A company is counted in a quarter when at least one of its reports has such an entry. Only the latest version of each report is used.

The words searched for each topic are:

  • Artificial intelligence: "artificial intelligence" or "AI"
  • Data centers: "data center" or "data centers"
  • Preemption of state laws: "preempt", "preemption" or "moratorium"
  • Export controls: "export control" or "export controls"
  • Semiconductors and chips: "semiconductor", "chips" or "CHIPS Act"
  • Energy and electricity: "energy", "electricity" or "grid"
  • Privacy: "privacy" or "data protection"
  • Copyright: "copyright" or "intellectual property"
  • Children and online safety: "children", "kids", "minors", "child safety", "COPPA" or "KOSA"
  • Section 230 and content moderation: "Section 230", "content moderation" or "intermediary liability"
  • Antitrust and competition: "antitrust" or "competition"
  • Crypto and digital assets: "crypto", "digital asset", "stablecoin" or "blockchain"
  • Tariffs: "tariff" or "tariffs"
  • Immigration and visas: "immigration", "visa" or "H-1B"
  • Quantum computing: "quantum"

These are simple word searches, and nobody has read each passage. A match can be incidental: "energy" also matches the Energy and Commerce Committee, and "chips" covers both semiconductors and the CHIPS Act. A report can raise a subject without using these words, so the counts are a floor. Dollars are reported for a whole report and cannot be split by topic.

Tracked companies are the 83 companies on this site's lobbying list that named at least one topic; their reported names are listed on the spending page. "All lobbying clients" counts every client name in the data, in every industry, after ignoring capitalization and spacing.
