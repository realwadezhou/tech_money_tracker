Tech Money/2026/About

About

Tech Money is a personal project to make tech-industry political giving easier to read.

What's on this site

Election-cycle figures are built from Federal Election Commission filings — the disclosures that campaigns, PACs, and party committees submit by law. The focus is contributions where the donor's listed employer matches a tracked tech company (Google, Meta, Nvidia, Anthropic, and so on). Company-linked totals reflect those matched contributions; company PAC giving is not separately attributed to the company.

The separate federal lobbying explorer uses Lobbying Disclosure Act reports organized by calendar year. It indexes issue passages and report counts; it does not estimate AI lobbying spending.

Why it's not trivial

The FEC publishes raw filings. Employers on those filings are free text — "Google," "Google LLC," "google inc," "goog," and "alphabet" all come in as different strings. Identifying tech-linked records means maintaining a lookup from employer strings to company names. Employer matching is one limit; receipt selection, refunds, routed and attributed gifts, and changing committee relationships also affect the totals.

What's missing

  • Employer matching is incomplete. Someone who wrote "Self-employed" or an unusual abbreviation of a tech employer won't be counted here.
  • The tracked-company list is curated, not exhaustive.
  • Contributions reported only as unitemized totals have no donor names or employers attached, so they cannot be matched here.
