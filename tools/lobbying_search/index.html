An issue entry is one topic paragraph inside a quarterly report; one report can have many. A client is the company or group paying for the lobbying (grouped by its name as written). "% of clients" is the safer measure: one lobbying firm pasting the same paragraph for 40 clients inflates entry counts more than client counts. Matching ignores capitalization. Whole-word mode also finds simple plurals ("tariff" finds "tariffs") but not other word forms ("regulate" won't find "regulation"). Click any point to see who said it and the actual text. A tracked company groups all the reviewed spellings of one company (and its subsidiaries); when you pick one, the chart counts its issue entries.

👆 Click a dot to see who said it that quarter, or click a phrase name above for all quarters.

Filers type client names by hand, so one company appears under several spellings. A tracked company already has its spellings reviewed (in data/reference/companies/lda_clients.csv). For anything else, type part of the name and tick the names that are really the company you mean; the chart adds up the ticked ones. Two kinds of money appear on reports: outside firms' income (a lobbying firm reports what this client paid it) and in-house expenses (the company reports its own lobbying costs). In-house expense totals usually already include what the company paid outside firms, so adding the two lines together can double count.
